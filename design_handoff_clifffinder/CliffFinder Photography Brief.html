Photography Brief

CliffFinder · what to shoot, license, and ship — for v1 launch

Purpose. CliffFinder lives or dies on its imagery. The whole product is "this place looks worth the trip." Ugly photos kill that. This document defines the photo set we need before launch, the technical specs, the mood, and the per-screen usage rules. Hand it to a stock buyer, a contracted photographer, or an internal asset team and they should be able to deliver without further questions.

1 · Image categories

Every photo in CliffFinder serves one of seven jobs. Each has its own composition rule.

CategoryWhere it appearsAspectComposition rule
Hero · Spot Spot Details photo carousel (4 per spot) 3:2 · 2400×1600 The cliff and the water visible in one frame. Wide enough to gauge height. Bottom 30% reads as cliff/water (not sky) so the title overlay has dark bed.
Card · Spot Map mini-cards, Logbook entries, Radar feed thumbs 4:5 · 1200×1500 Vertical crop of the hero. Subject centered or left-third (right side hosts the heart icon). Edges read clearly at 200px width.
Action Spot Details secondary photos, "Trending" chips, Sign-in hero 3:2 · 2400×1600 A jumper mid-air or on the edge, framed small inside the landscape (not portrait-style). Implies "people use this app," doesn't put faces front and center.
Detail Spot Details secondary photos (texture, water, rocks) 1:1 · 1200×1200 Close-ups: water surface, rock face, mossy ledge, depth marker. Adds texture; reduces "another mountain pic" fatigue.
Onboarding Sign-in hero, splash (skipped for v1 but reserved) 3:2 · 2400×1600 One signature image: a waterfall or a sunlit cliff over still water. Saturated, golden-hour. This is the brand image.
Avatar Profile, Radar friend rows 1:1 · 600×600 User-generated. Square, face-centered. App auto-crops. We supply 6 placeholder gradient initials for empty states.
Map tile mood Background under glass overlays n/a · ESRI Not photographed — sourced from ESRI World Imagery. Just verify our 5 launch spots have clean satellite coverage.

3 · Mood & reference

The aesthetic: natural, warm, slightly cinematic. Not commercial-stock, not GoPro-action. Think: a friend who's a good photographer, sending you their best shots from a weekend trip.

Hero · waterfall, forest light
This is the mood for the Sign-in screen. Saturated greens, mist, a hint of sun. No people in frame.
Spot · sea cliff
Cliff and water in one frame, height legible. Bottom third reads dark. Eagle Cliff template.
Spot · alpine quarry / lake
Pristine water, granite, mountains behind. The "Hidden Quarry" archetype.
Vista · panoramic
Wide landscape, atmospheric. Used as Logbook empty-state background and Vista Point hero.

4 · Technical specs

Format & sizing

  • Format: JPEG, quality 80, sRGB color profile.
  • Master size: 2400×1600 (3:2) or 2400×3000 (4:5). Long edge ≥ 2400px.
  • Two delivery resolutions: @2x (2400px long edge) and @1x (1200px long edge). The app picks based on device DPR.
  • Target weight: < 400 KB at @2x, < 150 KB at @1x. Use mozjpeg or squoosh.
  • EXIF: stripped except orientation. No GPS coordinates in production photos.

Naming convention

  • Spot heroes: spot-{id}-hero-{1..4}.jpg → e.g. spot-eagle-hero-1.jpg
  • Action: action-jumper-{1..3}.jpg
  • UI: ui-signin-hero.jpg, ui-empty-logbook.png

Color & tone

  • Lean warm — slight golden-hour bias. Avoid cold cyan-shifted shadows.
  • Greens should read as forest green (#2E7D32 territory), not lime or olive.
  • Water should look cold — muted teal/slate, not pool-blue.
  • Avoid HDR oversaturation. If it looks like a desktop wallpaper, it's wrong.

5 · Sourcing

Three legitimate paths

  1. Licensed stock. Unsplash+, Adobe Stock, Stocksy. Budget $30–80 per image, $200–600 for the v1 set. Fastest. Caveat: photos appear in other apps; we don't own them.
  2. Commissioned shoot. One photographer, one weekend, all 5 spots. Budget $1.5K–4K. Best result, exclusive rights. Requires actually being able to access these locations.
  3. User-submitted. Long-term direction. Once we have 50+ active users, photos come from the community via Spot submissions. Each submission needs a click-through license grant. NICE for v1.
Recommendation. Ship v1 on stock (path 1) — 6 must-have photos, ~$300 total. Within 60 days, commission a single weekend shoot covering all 5 launch spots (path 2). Begin user-submitted intake at month 3.

License hygiene

  • Every photo file has a sibling .license.txt with: source, photographer, license type, date acquired, link to receipt.
  • If using Unsplash free tier, credit photographer in the app's Settings → Credits screen — already in scope.
  • No Pinterest, no Google Image search, no "I'll just use this one." Ever.

6 · Do & don't

✓ Do

  • Show the cliff and the water in the same frame
  • Compose with a clear height cue (a person, a railing, a tree)
  • Use available light. Golden hour, overcast diffuse, dappled forest
  • Crop tight on detail shots — moss, water, granite
  • Leave room at edges for UI overlays (top 80px, bottom 120px)

✗ Don't

  • Center a single jumper mid-air filling the frame (looks like a GoPro ad)
  • Drone-top-down shots — they hide the height, which is the whole point
  • Recognizable faces without a release form
  • Overcast featureless gray skies — replace or recompose
  • Heavy filters or visible HDR halos

8 · Pre-launch checklist

  • ☐ All 6 MUST photos licensed and in /assets/photos/
  • ☐ Every photo has a .license.txt sibling
  • ☐ Photos compressed to under target file size (mozjpeg run)
  • ☐ Each photo manually checked at 200px (card view) — does the subject still read?
  • ☐ Each photo manually checked at 800px (hero) — does it look in-place, or like stock?
  • ☐ Settings → Credits screen lists every photographer + license
  • ☐ Avatar fallback gradients render for users with no photo
  • ☐ Image upload flow handles HEIC (iPhone default) → JPEG conversion
  • ☐ Photo failure / offline state shows a graceful gradient, not a broken icon
